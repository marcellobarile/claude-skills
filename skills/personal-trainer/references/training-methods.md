## 2. Periodization Models {#periodization}

### Linear Periodization (LP)
Best for: beginners, strength focus, peaking for competition.
- Each week, add small load increments (2.5 kg / 5 lbs on upper; 5 kg / 10 lbs on lower).
- Typical block: 4–6 weeks before reset or deload.
- Limitation: adaptation stalls quickly in intermediates.

### Daily Undulating Periodization (DUP)
Best for: intermediates, concurrent strength + hypertrophy, variety lovers.
- Within the same week, rotate rep ranges per session.
- Example 3-day DUP:
  - Day 1: 3–5 reps (strength)
  - Day 2: 8–12 reps (hypertrophy)
  - Day 3: 12–20 reps (metabolic / endurance)
- Auto-regulates fatigue by varying stimulus.

### Block Periodization
Best for: advanced athletes, competition prep.
- Accumulation block (3–4 wk): high volume, moderate intensity (65–75% 1RM)
- Intensification block (3–4 wk): moderate volume, high intensity (80–90%)
- Realization/Peaking block (1–2 wk): low volume, max intensity (90–100%)
- Followed by deload / transition.

### Wave Loading
Best for: strength days, neurological adaptation.
- Sets descend in reps and ascend in load across a "wave".
- Example: 6 reps @75% → 4 @82% → 2 @88%, repeat wave with slight upward shift.

## 3. Volume Landmarks (Hypertrophy) {#volume-landmarks}

Based on Mike Israetel's RP model. Per muscle group per week (in sets of ~8–20 reps):

| Volume | Definition | Typical Sets/wk |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| MV (Maintenance Volume) | Minimum to maintain muscle | 4–8 |
| MEV (Minimum Effective Volume) | Minimum to grow | 6–10 |
| MAV (Maximum Adaptive Volume) | Optimal growth range | 10–20 |
| MRV (Maximum Recoverable Volume) | Upper limit before overtraining | 16–26 |

**Programming rule**: Start near MEV. Add 1–2 sets per muscle per week across the
mesocycle. Deload when approaching MRV or when performance degrades.

Muscle groups and typical weekly training frequency:
- Large (quads, back, chest): 2–4× / wk
- Medium (hamstrings, delts, calves): 2–4× / wk
- Small (biceps, triceps): 2–6× / wk (recover faster)

## 4. Loading Schemes {#loading-schemes}

### RPE Scale (Rate of Perceived Exertion — Powerlifting scale)
- RPE 10 = absolute max, could not do another rep
- RPE 9 = 1 rep left in tank
- RPE 8 = 2 reps left
- RPE 7 = 3 reps left
- Use RPE to auto-regulate load. Beginner: RPE 6–8. Intermediate: 7–9. Advanced: 8–10.

### RIR (Reps In Reserve) — equivalent, simpler phrasing
- 0 RIR = failure
- 1 RIR = RPE 9
- 2 RIR = RPE 8
Hypertrophy: train to 0–3 RIR. Strength: 1–3 RIR on working sets.

### % 1RM Estimates (Epley formula for reference)
- 100%: 1 rep
- 90%: ~3–4 reps to failure
- 80%: ~8 reps to failure
- 70%: ~12–15 reps to failure
- 60%: ~20 reps to failure

### Progressive Overload Strategies
1. **Load progression**: increase weight when target reps achieved across all sets.
2. **Volume progression**: add a set before increasing load.
3. **Density progression**: same volume in less time (shorter rest).
4. **Technique progression**: same load, better form and range of motion.
5. **Rep range extension**: push into top of rep range before adding load.

## 5. Cardiovascular Training {#cardio}

### Zone 2 (aerobic base)
- Heart rate: ~60–70% HRmax, or "can hold a conversation" pace.
- Duration: 30–90 min per session.
- Goal: improve mitochondrial density, fat oxidation, aerobic base.
- Frequency: 3–5× / week for endurance goals; 1–2× as supplement for strength athletes.

### Zone 4–5 / HIIT
- Heart rate: 85–95% HRmax.
- Work intervals: 20 s – 4 min. Rest: 1:1 to 1:3 work:rest.
- Common protocols: Tabata (20s/10s × 8), 4 × 4 min at VO2max pace.
- Use sparingly with heavy strength programs to avoid interference effect.

### Concurrent training (cardio + strength same program)
- Minimize interference: separate cardio and strength by ≥6 h, or do cardio after
  strength in the same session.
- Prefer low-impact cardio (cycling, rowing, swimming) to reduce leg fatigue overlap
  with squat/deadlift programming.
- Limit HIIT to ≤2× / wk when running significant strength volume.

## 6. Sport-Specific Conditioning {#sport-specific}

### General approach
1. Identify the primary energy system(s) of the sport.
2. Map movement patterns (unilateral, bilateral, rotational, overhead, etc.).
3. Identify injury-prone areas for that sport.
4. Structure off-season (GPP), pre-season (SPP), and in-season (maintenance).

### Energy system mapping
| Sport | Primary system | Secondary |
|-------|---------------|-----------|
| Sprint / power | Phosphocreatine (0–10 s) | Glycolytic |
| Team sports (football, basketball) | Glycolytic + phosphocreatine | Aerobic |
| Distance running / cycling / swimming | Aerobic | Glycolytic |
| Martial arts / combat | Glycolytic | Aerobic |
| Rock climbing | Glycolytic + strength | Aerobic |
| Weightlifting / powerlifting | Phosphocreatine | Strength |

### Key injury-prone areas by sport (consider in programming)
- Running: hip flexors, ITB, Achilles, plantar fascia → hip stability, calf work
- Cycling: knee (patellar), lower back → glute strengthening, hip flexor mobility
- Swimming: rotator cuff, shoulder → external rotation work, posterior chain
- Football/basketball: ACL, ankle → landing mechanics, single-leg stability
- Tennis/racquet: lateral elbow, shoulder → forearm + rotator cuff prehab
- Combat sports: neck, ribs, spine → bracing, anti-rotation core
- CrossFit: shoulder, lumbar, knees → technique-first loading, shoulder health

### Peaking for competition
- Last 2 weeks: reduce volume 40–60%, maintain or slightly reduce intensity.
- Taper length: 7–14 days for strength/power; 14–21 days for endurance.
- Keep a few "race pace" or "competition intensity" efforts to maintain sharpness.

## 7. Deload Protocols {#deload}

### When to deload
- Scheduled: every 3–6 weeks depending on intensity and experience level.
- Reactive: when multiple signs appear (persistent soreness > 72h, performance
  decline 2+ sessions in a row, sleep disruption, mood/motivation drop).

### Deload types
1. **Volume deload** (most common): cut sets by 40–50%, keep load the same.
   - Example: 4 × 8 → 2 × 8 at same weight.
2. **Intensity deload**: keep sets same, reduce load 20–40%.
3. **Full deload**: 3–5 days of only light movement (walks, mobility, swimming).

### Post-deload
Expect a brief period of "supercompensation" — performance often jumps after a
quality deload. Program the first post-deload session slightly below pre-deload
volume to ease back in.

## 8. Recovery Principles {#recovery}

### Sleep
- 7–9 h optimal for most adults.
- Chronic sleep restriction < 6 h significantly impairs strength gains and recovery.
- Recommend consistent sleep/wake schedule. Flag if user reports poor sleep — adjust
  training volume/intensity accordingly.

### Nutrition basics (general principles only — defer to RD for specifics)
- Protein: 1.6–2.2 g/kg body weight/day for hypertrophy and strength.
- Caloric surplus (~200–400 kcal/day) for lean muscle gain.
- Caloric deficit for fat loss; keep protein high to preserve muscle.
- Carbohydrates fuel performance — don't eliminate for strength/power athletes.
- Hydration: monitor urine color (pale yellow = adequate).

### Active recovery
- Light movement on rest days (walking, swimming, yoga) improves circulation without
  adding training stress.
- Foam rolling / soft tissue work: ~5–10 min pre/post session for targeted areas.

## 9. Injury-Aware Programming {#injury}

### General principles
- **Never train through acute pain.** Soreness ≠ pain. Sharp, localized, or joint
  pain = stop and assess.
- **Regress, don't stop.** For most injuries, some movement is better than complete
  rest. Find a pain-free variation.
- **Prehabilitation**: include targeted accessory work for injury-prone structures
  (rotator cuff, hip abductors, hip flexors, posterior chain).

### Common modifications
| Injury | Avoid | Alternative |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| Lower back | Loaded flexion, heavy deadlift | Romanian DL, trap bar DL, hip hinge variation |
| Knee (anterior) | Deep squat, leg extension | Box squat, step-up, leg press with reduced ROM |
| Shoulder | Overhead press, upright row | Neutral-grip DB press, landmine press |
| Elbow (lateral epicondyle) | Wrist curls, pronated pull | Hammer curl, neutral pull-down |
| Hip flexor | Sprint, high knee | Hip hinge focus, deadlift variations |
| Achilles / plantar | Running, jump landing | Swimming, cycling, upper body focus |

### Red flags — refer to physician / physio
- Pain that radiates (nerve involvement)
- Swelling, bruising, instability in a joint
- Pain that worsens with rest or at night
- Symptoms that don't improve in 2–3 weeks with modified training
